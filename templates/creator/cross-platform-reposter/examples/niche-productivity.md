Three patterns we keep seeing — across surprisingly different domains:

**Pattern 1 — The dashboard climbs, the real result slips.** A team ships a change that lifts every surface metric. A few weeks later, the thing they actually cared about has gotten worse. The metric was graded on shape; the outcome was graded on substance.

**Pattern 2 — The work gets noisier as it gets better-graded.** Each iteration scores higher on the proxy and adds more surface area. Investigation reveals the grader is rewarding answers that look thorough rather than answers that resolve the problem.

The remediation in all three cases is the same. Replace the surface-flavoured metric with an outcome grader. Treat the short-term dashboard regression as a cost of moving to the right metric, not a setback.

## Platform Adaptations

### Linkedin
- **Length**: 1300-2200 chars; multi-paragraph with optional 1 numbered list.
- **Tone**: Professional + practical; first-person observational framing lands well.
- **Hashtag posture**: 0-2 niche-relevant hashtags, only at the very end.
- **CTA shape**: End on a concrete recommendation, not a generic question.

### Threads
- **Length**: 280-500 chars per post; one or two posts max.
- **Tone**: Casual + conversational; mobile-first phrasing.
- **Hashtag posture**: 0 hashtags; community tags only when tied to a niche group.
- **CTA shape**: End on a question or reply hook to invite responses.

### Newsletter
- **Length**: 600-1200 word section; one inline diagram OK.
- **Tone**: Thoughtful + structured; cite at least one external source.
- **Hashtag posture**: None — newsletter readers experience hashtags as noise.
- **CTA shape**: End with a 'next-week's post will cover X' tease or a reply prompt.
